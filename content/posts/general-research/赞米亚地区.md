这一术语 **东南亚地块** [[ 1 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-1)该理论由人类学家让·米肖于1997年提出。 [[ 2 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-2)本文旨在探讨居住在亚洲大陆东南部海拔约300米（1000英尺）以上地区的人类社会，其范围不仅限于传统意义上的 [东南亚大陆 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia "东南亚大陆")高地。本文涉及横跨十个国家的高原地区： [中国西南地区 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_China "中国西南地区")、 [印度东北部 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India "印度东北部")、 [孟加拉国 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "孟加拉国")的所有高原地区 [东部，以及缅甸（原名缅甸） ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_(Burma) "缅甸（缅甸）")、 [泰国 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "泰国")、 [越南 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "越南")、 [老挝 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "老挝")、 [柬埔寨 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "柬埔寨")、 [马来西亚半岛 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia "马来西亚半岛")和 [台湾 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "台湾")。这些地区内的原住民人口约1亿，还不包括过去几个世纪以来从周边低地人口迁徙至此定居的移民。

东南亚地块的概念在地理上与范·申德尔 [的佐米亚概念的东部部分重叠。 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#Zomia)在2002年提出 [[ 3 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-3)虽然它在地理上与政治学家 [詹姆斯·C·斯科特 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_C._Scott "詹姆斯·C·斯科特")在 2009 年所称的佐米亚地区重叠。 [[ 4 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-4)虽然“佐米亚”的概念强调了对该高地地区的历史和政治理解，但东南亚地块更恰当地被称作一个地方或一个 [社会空间 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_space "社交空间")。

[![东南亚地块（红色）与喜马拉雅地块（黄色）相邻[5]](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/GENERAL-Massif_2May2010.jpg/250px-GENERAL-Massif_2May2010.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:GENERAL-Massif_2May2010.jpg)

西藏世界不属于该山脉，因为它有自己的逻辑：一个中央集权、宗教和谐的核心，以及悠久而独特的政治存在，使其处于“封建”和帝国的范畴，而历史上与该山脉相关的社会很少（如果有的话）发展到这种程度。 [[ 6 ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-6)

为了更深入地描述高原地区的独特性，可以纳入一系列核心因素：历史、语言、宗教、传统社会结构、经济以及与低地国家的政治关系。高原社会的独特之处或许远超其共同点：广袤的生态系统、边缘化的地位以及各种形式的从属关系。高原地区横跨六大语系（ [南亚语系 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages "南亚语系")、 [苗瑶语系 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong%E2%80%93Mien_languages "苗瑶语")、 [克拉傣 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kra%E2%80%93Dai_languages "克拉-傣语")语系、 [汉藏语系 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan_languages "汉藏语系")、 [印欧语系 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages "印欧语系")、 [南岛语系 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages "南岛语系")），但没有哪个语系占据绝对多数。在宗教方面，一些族群信奉万物有灵论 [， ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animist "万物有灵论者")另一些信奉 [佛教 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist "佛教徒")，一些信奉 [基督教 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "基督徒")，相当一部分人认同 [道教 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoist "道教")和 [儒家 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucian "儒家")价值观，回族信奉 [伊斯兰教 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim "穆斯林")， [库基钦语系的 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki-Chin_languages "库基-钦语")[贝内·梅纳什人 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bnei_Menashe "贝内·梅纳什")信奉 [犹太教 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "犹太教")，而大多数社会则呈现出复杂的 [宗教融合 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism "合一")现象。纵观历史，地方族群之间的世仇和频繁的敌对行动，正是文化多元性的体现。 [[ 7 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-7)该地区从未在政治上统一过，无论是作为一个帝国，还是作为几个交战王国共享的领土，甚至连一个拥有协调政治制度的区域都没有。这里存在着各种不同的习惯性政治组织形式，主要 [以血统 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineal_descendant "直系后裔")为基础，而非“ [封建 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudal "封建")”。 [[ 8 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-8)早已存在。

与喜马拉雅山脉周边及世界各地的其他跨国高原地区一样，东南亚地块在历史、经济和文化方面都处于边缘化和分散状态。因此，它似乎缺乏作为 [亚洲研究 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_studies "亚洲研究")一个有前景的细分区域的必要重要性。然而，在研究跨界和 [边缘化社会 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_exclusion "Social exclusion")时，重新思考以国家为基础的研究方法至关重要。

Inquiries on the ground throughout the massif show that these peoples share a sense of being different from the national majorities, a sense of geographical remoteness, and a state of marginality that is connected to political and economic distance from regional seats of power. In cultural terms, these highland societies are like a cultural mosaic with contrasting colours, rather than an integrated picture in harmonized shades – what Terry Rambo, talking from a Vietnam perspective, has dubbed "a psychedelic nightmare".[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-9)

Historically, these highlands have been used by lowland empires as reserves of resources (including slaves), and as buffer spaces between their domains.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-10)

**Zomia** is a [geographical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic "Geographic") term [coined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism "Neologism") in 2002 by historian [Willem van Schendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Willem_van_Schendel_(historian)&action=edit&redlink=1 "Willem van Schendel (historian) (page does not exist)") of the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam "University of Amsterdam")[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-12) to refer to the huge mass of mainland [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia#Geography "Southeast Asia") that has historically been beyond the control of [governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government "Government") based in the [population centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_center "Population center") of the [lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland "Lowland").[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-13) It largely overlaps with the geographical extent of the Southeast Asian Massif, although the exact boundaries of Zomia differ among scholars:[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-14) all would include the [highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_(geography) "Highland (geography)") of north [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina "Indochina") (north [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Vietnam "Geography of Vietnam") and all [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Laos "Geography of Laos")), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Highlands "Thai Highlands"), the [Shan Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_Hills "Shan Hills") of northern [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), and the [mountains of Southwest China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountains_of_Southwest_China "Mountains of Southwest China"); some extend the region as far west as [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau "Tibetan Plateau"), [Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India "Northeast India"), [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Pakistan "Geography of Pakistan"), and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"). These areas share a common elevated, rugged [terrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrain "Terrain"), and have been the home of [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group "Minority group") that have preserved their local cultures by residing far from [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_(polity) "State (polity)") control and influence. Other scholars have used the term to discuss the similar ways that Southeast Asian governments have handled minority groups.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-15)

The name is from "[Zomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zo_people "Zo people")", a term for [highlanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_people "Hill people") common to several related [Tibeto-Burman languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages "Tibeto-Burman languages") spoken in the India-Bangladesh-[Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar") border area.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-16)

Professor [James C. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_C._Scott "James C. Scott") of [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") used the concept of Zomia in his 2009 book [*The Art of Not Being Governed: An Anarchist History of Upland Southeast Asia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_Not_Being_Governed "The Art of Not Being Governed") to argue that the continuity of the ethnic cultures living there provides a counter-narrative to the traditional story about [modernity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernity "Modernity"): namely, that once people are exposed to the conveniences of modern technology and the modern state, they will [assimilate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation "Cultural assimilation"). Rather, the [tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe "Tribe") in Zomia are conscious [refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees "Refugees") from state rule and state-centered economies. From his preface:

> [Hill tribes] seen from the valley kingdoms as "our living ancestors", "what we were like before we discovered [wet-rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet-rice "Wet-rice") cultivation, [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), and civilization [are on the contrary] best understood as runaway, fugitive, maroon communities who have, over the course of two millennia, been fleeing the oppressions of state-making projects in the valleys—slavery, conscription, taxes, [corvée labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corv%C3%A9e "Corvée"), epidemics, and warfare.

Scott goes on to add that Zomia is the biggest remaining area of earth whose inhabitants have not been completely absorbed by nation-states, although that time is coming to an end. While Zomia is exceptionally diverse linguistically, the languages spoken in the hills are distinct from those spoken in the plains. Kinship structures, at least formally, also distinguish the hills from the lowlands. Hill societies do produce "a surplus", but they do not use that surplus to support kings and monks. Distinctions of status and wealth abound in the hills, as in the valleys. The difference is that in the valleys they tend to be enduring, while in the hills they are both unstable and geographically confined.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-17)

### Differing perspectives

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Southeast_Asian_Massif&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Differing perspectives")]

中解释了在称呼佐米亚居民时所使用的语言所带来的诸多困境 *让·米肖在他的《东南亚地块民族历史词典》* 。 [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-18)佐米亚人常被称为“民族少数民族”，米肖认为这两个词都存在争议。关于“ [民族 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation "国家")”一词，米肖指出，东南亚地块的居民实际上是跨国的，因为许多群体跨越多个国家。米肖认为，“ [少数民族 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group "少数族裔群体")”一词也并非描述该群体的恰当方式，因为其人口规模庞大。米肖甚至认为，“群体”一词也存在问题，因为它带有社群和“社会凝聚力”的含义，而并非所有群体都具备这些特质。 [[ 19 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-19)[[ 20 ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-20)

2010 年， [*《全球历史杂志》* ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Global_History "全球历史杂志")出版了一期特刊，题为“佐米亚及其他”。 [[ 21 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-21)在本期中，当代东南亚历史学家和社会科学家对斯科特的论点作出了回应。例如，尽管东南亚问题专家 [维克多·利伯曼 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Lieberman "维克多·利伯曼")[[ 22 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-22)他同意高地人民根据他们遇到的政治和自然环境创造了自己的社会世界，但他也认为斯科特的文献非常薄弱，尤其是缺乏缅甸语资料，并表示这不仅削弱了斯科特的几个关键论点，而且还使他关于佐米亚的一些其他理论受到质疑。

此外，利伯曼认为斯科特高估了人力在军事胜利中的重要性。斯科特的论点主要基于低地国家征服高地的努力，而利伯曼则指出海上贸易同样是重要的影响因素。

利伯曼还指出，斯科特的分析中未包含的例子也需要考虑。斯科特坚信，这种文化的形成是一种防御机制，是对周围政治和社会环境的一种反应。然而，利伯曼认为， [婆罗洲 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "婆罗洲")/ [加里曼丹 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan "加里曼丹")的高地居民与佐米亚人拥有几乎相同的文化特征，例如地方语言的传播和 [刀耕火种的耕作方式 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shifting_cultivation "轮耕")，而这些特征的形成都与低地掠夺性国家的统治无关。 [[ 23 ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-23)

最近， [汤姆·布拉斯 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Brass "汤姆·布拉斯")对斯科特的说法提出了质疑。 [[ 24 ] ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-24)布拉斯认为，将东南亚高地描述为“排斥国家”的“避难/庇护区”，并声称人们自愿迁徙至此，这种说法是不正确的。他认为，这是一种符合“新”民粹主义后现代主义的理想化，但缺乏民族志证据的支持。后者表明，人们既非自愿迁徙至高地（而是被迫离开山谷），而且一旦迁徙至高地，他们也并非脱离了低地国家的管辖。因此，在这样的环境中，他们根本谈不上拥有自主权和安全感。

[爱德华·斯特林厄姆 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Stringham "爱德华·斯特林汉姆")和卡莱布·J·迈尔斯分析了东南亚社会的历史和人类学证据，得出结论：这些社会数千年来一直避免建立国家。斯特林厄姆进一步分析了他们用来避免、抵制和阻止潜在国家的制度。他进一步得出结论：像“佐米亚”这样的无国家社会，利用其地理位置、特定的生产方式以及对国家的文化抵制，成功地抵制了国家的出现。 [[ 25 ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Massif#cite_note-Stringham-25)
